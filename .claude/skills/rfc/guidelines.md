# RFC Writing Guidelines

## Tone and Approach

- **Lead with the "why"** before the "what" and "how"
- **Be concrete and specific** -- vague proposals get vague feedback
- **Use diagrams** where they clarify (describe them in text/ASCII if the user will add visuals later)
- **Call out trade-offs explicitly** -- every design decision has them
- **Keep it proportionate** -- a 2-week project doesn't need a 20-page RFC
- **Write for the reviewer** -- assume they're smart but don't have your context
- **Mark uncertainties** -- it's better to flag "I'm not sure about X" than to present a guess as fact
- **Distinguish facts from opinions** -- be clear about what you know vs. what you're proposing

## Adapting to Complexity

**Lightweight RFC** (team-scoped, moderate complexity):
- Summary, Context, Goals/Non-Goals, Proposed Design, Alternatives, Open Questions
- Skip or minimize: Rollout Plan, Success Metrics, Cross-Cutting Concerns

**Full RFC** (cross-team, high complexity, high risk):
- All sections, thoroughly filled out
- Consider adding: approver sign-off fields, timeline/milestones, capacity/resource needs

**Decision Record** (documenting a decision already made):
- Summary, Context, Decision, Consequences, Alternatives Considered
- Lighter format focused on "what was decided and why" rather than soliciting feedback
